Where the eye goes

Chris: “The main pages look too monochromatic… everything falling into the background. It would be nice for the right things to be the center of attention.” Three answers, on the same Today screen, with the same real content. Nothing here ships until it is picked.

  • 1

    One hue does every job. The accent marks eyebrows, numbers, buttons, meters, the active tab, borders and progress. When a single colour means “important”, “interactive”, “active” and “decorative” at once, it stops meaning any of them.

  • 2

    Every surface sits at the same depth. --ink and --surface are a few points apart, and cards separate by a 1px border rather than by tone. Nothing is foreground, so nothing recedes — which is exactly the flat feeling.

  • 3

    The subject colours are quarantined. Twelve real hues already ship — jade, ember, gold, rose — and they appear only on subject tiles, book spines and the new week strip. The screens where she actually works never see them.

What I would actually do

B for the structure, A for the colour, and C only for the quiz. Depth fixes the flatness without adding a single new hue, so it costs nothing in restraint — and it makes “the thing to do now” legible even on a day with no colour to give it. A then spends colour where it already carries meaning: the subject. That keeps the accent for what is tappable, which is the job it should have had all along.

C is the risk. It is genuinely more engaging, and on a quiet day it has nothing to shout about — a loud empty card is worse than a quiet full one. It belongs where there is always one obvious next action: inside a unit, not on Today.

Everything above is built from the apps' own tokens, so whatever is chosen moves across as CSS, not as a rewrite. The subject hues are SUBJECT_PALETTE already in both apps; the depth levels are --deep, --surface and --raised, all of which already exist and two of which are barely used.
